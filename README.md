Problem Title

SIH 26033: Multiple intermediaries reduce farmers' earnings and increase consumer prices.

Proposed Solution

1. Detailed explanation of the proposed solution

A mobile app and website where farmers and FPOs list their produce with quantity, quality and price.
Consumers, retailers, hotels and bulk buyers browse and order directly from them.
Payments go straight to the farmer's bank account through UPI.
Pickup, delivery and storage are arranged inside the same platform.
AI predicts demand and suggests the best delivery routes.

2. How it addresses the problem

Removes middlemen, so farmers earn more and consumers pay less.
Shows live market prices, so farmers know a fair rate.
Shared logistics cuts transport cost and spoilage.
Demand forecasts tell farmers what to grow and sell, reducing waste.

3. Innovation and uniqueness

One platform covering marketplace, logistics and AI.
Support for local languages and voice, so farmers with low literacy can use it.
Pooled delivery that combines orders from nearby farmers into one trip.
Quality grading and ratings to build trust between buyers and farmers.



Technical Approach

1. Technologies to be used

Frontend: React.js (web), Flutter or React Native (mobile)
Backend: Node.js or Python (Django/FastAPI)
Database: PostgreSQL / MongoDB
AI/ML: Python, scikit-learn, TensorFlow (demand forecasting); Google OR-Tools (route optimization)
Payments: UPI / Razorpay
Maps and tracking: Google Maps API
Cloud: AWS or Azure
Language support: Voice input and regional language translation

2. Methodology and process

Farmer or FPO registers and lists produce.
Buyer searches, orders and pays.
The AI engine forecasts demand and sets a fair price range.
The system assigns the nearest vehicle and the best route.
Produce is delivered and tracked live.
Payment is released to the farmer after delivery.

Feasibility and Viability

1. Feasibility of the idea

Most farmers already have smartphones, UPI and internet access.
The technologies are mature and low cost.
It fits government efforts such as e-NAM, FPO support and Digital India.

2. Potential challenges and risks

Farmers may find it hard to adopt a new app.
Quality may vary, and perishable produce can spoil in transit.
Rural logistics are costly and patchy.
Buyers may not trust a new platform.

3. Strategies to overcome them

Simple voice-based app in local languages, with training through FPOs and local agents.
Quality checks, ratings and return policies to build trust.
Pooled deliveries and local collection hubs to cut costs.
Start with a pilot in one district, then scale up.




Impact and Benefits

1. Potential impact on the target audience

Farmers/FPOs: higher income, faster payment, direct market access.
Consumers: fresher produce at lower prices.
Bulk buyers: reliable supply and transparent pricing.

2. Benefits of the solution

Social: empowers small farmers, builds trust and supports rural communities.
Economic: fewer intermediaries, fairer prices, and new jobs in logistics and collection hubs.
Environmental: less food waste, shorter routes and lower fuel use.
